Benchmark

Every policy faces the same enumerated tasks in the same order, drawn from a frozen test split. oracle-replay is not a competitor: it replays the recorded expert through the same action codec a policy must use, so its score is the ceiling any policy could reach.

Compounding error

The policy is handed an expert prefix and must supply the last k actions itself. Larger k is a harder task.

Which parts are hard

Mean progress within each family. The suite mixes families of very different difficulty, and a single number over all of them cannot say whether a policy has learned to build or has learned one shape.

Where each policy stops

Progress is prefix-scored: a milestone missed zeroes every later rung too, so the rung a policy stops at is the whole story of its score. Each bar is the share of episodes that reached that rung, and the marked rung is where the most were lost.

Building versus finishing

A BUILD task is a sentence and nothing else. A COMPLETE task hands the policy an expert prefix and asks for the last k actions. The headline score is the mean of both, so read them apart before reading them together.

Where the suite is won

The leaderboard is a mean over every task. Two policies with the same mean can be solving disjoint halves of the suite, and an average cannot tell them apart. One column per task, shaded by how many of the seven milestones the policy reached, ordered BUILD first and then COMPLETE by how many trailing actions the policy had to supply.

Paired comparisons

Bootstrap intervals on the per-task difference, recomputed from the traces in this bundle. An interval spanning zero means the benchmark cannot separate the two policies.

Behavioural cloning

Held out by design rather than by step. Splitting on steps would put an action from the same trajectory on both sides and inflate the number.

Loss

PPO

Clipped surrogate with a KL anchor back to the cloned policy. Reward is dense over milestones.

How the actions were refused

Every rejected action across all 76 tasks, reduced to the failure it describes. A policy that jams fails the same way over and over; one that flails fails many ways once.

Mistakes, or a stop

A refusal rate says how much of an episode was wasted, not whether the waste was scattered or one unbroken run at the end. Scattered refusals are a policy making mistakes; an unbroken tail is a policy that has stopped and is still being asked for actions.

What the policies did

The leaderboard says a policy scored 0.458. It does not say what it did. One BUILD task per family, every policy's episode laid against the expert's: BUILD starts from nothing, so the whole sequence is the policy's own, where a COMPLETE task is mostly replayed expert prefix.

What the policy uses

Each condition corrupts one input and re-runs the identical suite. shuffled-req pairs a design with another design's requirement; blank-req removes the text; no-mask drops the action-legality mask.
